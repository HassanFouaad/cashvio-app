A **bundle** is one product your customer buys once, made up of other products you already sell. Example: a gift set with two mugs and a bag of coffee. The bundle has its own price and photo, but stock comes from the items inside it.

## Bundle, Extra, or Discount?

| Question | Use |
|----------|-----|
| Does the customer pick add-ons on the same item? (extra shot, gift wrap) | **Extras** add to the price without touching stock |
| Do you want a lower price on one SKU for a limited time? | **Catalogue discounts** mark a variant, product, or category down on a schedule |
| Do you sell a fixed set of products together as one line? | **Bundles** take stock from every component when one bundle sells |

Extras stay on one SKU. Discounts change the price. Bundles combine separate products into one sellable item.

## Create a Bundle

1. Open **Catalogue > Products** and pick a product, or create a new one.
2. A bundle product has **exactly one variant**. Turn **inventory tracking off** on that product. The bundle itself holds no stock.
3. On the product details page, open the **Bundle contents** card and click **Make this a bundle** (or **Edit contents** if it already is one).
4. Add each component: search for a product variant, set **Quantity per bundle** (how many units of that item go into one bundle), and reorder rows for display.
5. Choose a pricing mode and save.

## How Stock Works

A bundle never has its own quantity on the shelf. **Sellable now** is calculated from the components:

- For each trackable component, divide available stock by **quantity per bundle** and round down.
- The bundle can sell as many complete sets as the **lowest** of those numbers.
- If every component is non-trackable, sellable quantity is **Unlimited**.

The card also shows **Enough for N** beside each component so you can see which item is the bottleneck.

<Callout type="info">
  Bundles do not appear in stocktake or the low-stock list, because they hold no stock of their own. Watch the component products instead.
</Callout>

## Pricing Modes

| Mode | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| **Set the price myself** | You type the bundle selling price directly |
| **Calculate from components** | Cashvio totals component list prices, applies your discount percentage, and writes the result as the bundle price |

Calculated prices **do not** auto-update when component prices change later. If the component total drifts from the bundle price, the portal shows a banner with **Recalculate** so you can refresh the price in one click.

## Sales, Returns, and Profit

- **Selling one bundle** deducts stock from **every** component in the quantities you defined.
- **Returning one bundle** puts the same component quantities back when the return is restocked.
- **Profit** on the bundle line uses the sum of component costs at sale time, so margin stays accurate even if you change the recipe later.

Past orders keep a snapshot of what was inside each bundle, so receipts and order history stay correct after you edit contents.

## Where Bundles Appear

| Surface | What customers and staff see |
|---------|------------------------------|
| **New Sale** | Bundle label on the tile; stock follows derived availability |
| **POS ticket** | One line with a short "Contains N items" note |
| **Receipts** | Bundle name plus indented component lines |
| **Product list** | Bundle marker and derived stock |
| **Online store** | Bundle badge on cards, "What's inside" on the product page, contents note in the cart |
| **Meta catalog feed** | Availability follows derived stock, not "always in stock" |

## Limitations

These rules are intentional in this release:

1. **No nesting**: a bundle cannot contain another bundle.
2. **One variant only**: two colourways of the same set are two separate bundle products.
3. **No stocktake row**: bundles do not appear in stocktake or low-stock alerts; track the components.
4. **No CSV import**: you cannot create bundles through product import in this release. Build them in the product details page.
5. **No dead-stock row**: the dead-stock report ranks products by stock movement. A bundle has none, so it never appears there.
